## 1. Product Strategy

### 1.1 Mission

> **To give every content creator access to the data-driven content strategy tools that were previously reserved for large media companies — without ever compromising the originality or integrity of their creative work.**

### 1.2 Vision

ViralScopes.io becomes the standard intelligence infrastructure for professional content creation. Every professional creator — from a solo YouTuber to a 50-person media company — uses ViralScopes as the foundation of their content strategy, the way a professional marketer uses Google Analytics.

**Five-year target:** £10M+ ARR, multi-platform intelligence, developer ecosystem, enterprise category leadership.

### 1.3 Value Proposition

| Audience | Core value delivered |
|---|---|
| **Independent creators** | Replace 5+ hours of weekly manual competitor research with a 30-minute Monday morning review. Get original content ideas grounded in what is actually working in their niche. |
| **Agencies** | Deliver data-backed content strategy briefings to all clients in 3 hours instead of 20. Scale strategy delivery without scaling headcount. |
| **Brand content teams** | Justify content investment decisions with objective Viral Score data. Benchmark against competitors systematically. Build data-backed content calendars. |
| **Media companies** | Systematic competitive intelligence at scale. RBAC-controlled access for each editorial team. API integration with internal BI tools. |

### 1.4 Competitive Positioning

ViralScopes is positioned as the **intelligent, ethical content intelligence platform** — the tool that understands *why* content goes viral rather than just tracking what has already gone viral.

```
                        HIGH INTELLIGENCE (WHY)
                               │
                               │
              ViralScopes ●   │
                               │
───────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────── HIGH SCALE
  (individual creator)         │                  (enterprise)
                               │
    TubeBuddy ●   VidIQ ●     │   Social Blade ●
                               │
                               │
                        LOW INTELLIGENCE (WHAT)
```

**Positioning statement:** ViralScopes is the only content intelligence platform that analyses the structural patterns behind viral performance — hook types, narrative arcs, title formulas, thumbnail psychology — and turns those patterns into original, ethically generated content guidance.

### 1.5 Unique Selling Propositions (USPs)

| USP | Why it matters | Defensibility |
|---|---|---|
| **Proprietary Viral Score** | Single, transparent, explainable 0–100 score from 9 signals | Algorithmic moat; improves with data |
| **Structural pattern analysis** | Analyses hook types, title formulas, narrative arcs — not just view counts | No competitor does this at this depth |
| **Ethical AI framework** | Generates original recommendations; never reproduces creator work | Trust differentiator; legal risk protection |
| **Trend classification** | Emerging / evergreen / declining — not just "trending" | Helps creators act with urgency and confidence |
| **Opportunity Engine** | Demand × growth ÷ competition ranking of untapped niches | Proprietary data combination |
| **Multi-channel alerts** | Email, Discord, Slack, Telegram, webhook — all in one platform | Workflow integration reduces switching cost |
| **Prompt versioning** | AI prompts versioned in DB; tunable without code deployment | Operational differentiator for quality |

## 2. Customer Segments

### 2.1 Segment Overview

| Segment | Size (global) | Willingness to pay | MVP priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent creators (10k–1M subs) | ~5M | £39–£89/month | Primary |
| Creator agencies (managing 3+ clients) | ~500k businesses | £249+/month | Primary |
| In-house brand content teams | ~2M teams | £89–£249/month | Secondary |
| Digital media companies | ~100k companies | £249–£3k/month | Secondary |
| Solo creators (< 10k subs) | ~45M | £0–£39/month | Free tier |
| Enterprise media groups | ~10k organisations | £1k–£5k/month | v2+ |

### 2.2 Individual Creators

**Profile:** Full-time or serious part-time YouTubers managing their own channels. Typically 10,000–1,000,000 subscribers. Solo or very small team (1–3 people).

**Pain points:**
- Spending 5–10 hours per week manually researching competitors
- No systematic way to understand which structural choices drive performance
- Discovering trends after they have already peaked
- Making content decisions based on intuition rather than data

**Goals:**
- Reduce research time to < 30 minutes per week
- Understand why their best videos worked so they can replicate the structure
- Identify emerging topics 2–4 weeks before competitors

**Buying motivations:**
- Time saving is the primary driver ("I get those hours back")
- Competitive advantage is the secondary driver ("I see what's coming before others do")
- Price sensitivity is moderate — if it helps them earn more from their channel, £39–£89/month is easy to justify

**Decision process:** Typically self-serve. Discovers via YouTube/search, signs up for free tier, converts within 30 days if they experience a clear "aha moment" (seeing a high Viral Score prediction proven correct, or finding an opportunity that leads to a successful video).

---

### 2.3 Creator Agencies

**Profile:** Agencies managing content strategy for 3–20 creator clients. Teams of 3–25 people. Revenue from retainer contracts with creators.

**Pain points:**
- Content strategy briefs take 2–3 hours per client per week to produce manually
- Difficult to justify strategy recommendations without data that clients can see
- Competitive intelligence is ad hoc and inconsistent across team members
- Onboarding a new client requires significant manual research setup time

**Goals:**
- Deliver better briefs faster — 8 client briefs in 3 hours instead of 20
- Differentiate agency services with data-backed recommendations
- Scale the agency without proportional headcount growth
- Create a competitive moat by systematising what others do manually

**Buying motivations:**
- ROI is very clear: if ViralScopes saves 17 hours of strategist time per week at £30/hour, that's £510/week saved — far more than the subscription cost
- Agencies also use ViralScopes as a competitive differentiator to attract new clients

**Decision process:** Often made by the agency founder or head of strategy. May require a team trial. Budgets managed professionally with company cards.

---

### 2.4 In-House Brand & Marketing Teams

**Profile:** Marketing teams at consumer brands managing a YouTube channel as part of their content marketing strategy. Teams of 2–8 people dedicated to video content.

**Pain points:**
- Cannot justify content investment decisions to senior leadership without data
- YouTube Analytics only shows internal performance — no competitive benchmarking
- Content calendar is built on instinct, not evidence
- Trend discovery is reactive — they produce content about topics after the peak

**Goals:**
- Produce data-backed content calendars
- Benchmark against competitors with objective metrics
- Build executive reporting that demonstrates content ROI
- Move from reactive to proactive on trend discovery

**Buying motivations:**
- Accountability to leadership: having data makes the case for content investment
- Competitive awareness: knowing when a competitor's content breaks out
- Efficiency: reducing the weekly hours spent on manual competitive research

**Decision process:** Usually needs sign-off from a marketing manager or director. Budget comes from a team or department budget. Proof-of-concept period (trial) is important.

---

### 2.5 Digital Media Companies & Publishers

**Profile:** Digital-first publishers with YouTube presences as audience development channels. Managing multiple channels and tracking many competitor channels. Teams of 5–50 people touching YouTube content.

**Pain points:**
- Monitoring 50+ competitor channels manually is impossible
- Different editorial teams have different intelligence needs (politics, sport, culture, tech)
- No systematic way to measure own YouTube content against industry benchmarks
- Cannot pipe YouTube intelligence data into existing internal BI tools

**Goals:**
- Systematic competitor monitoring at scale
- Team-level access control so each editorial team sees relevant intelligence
- API integration with internal data infrastructure
- Trend intelligence to brief editorial teams on emerging topics

**Buying motivations:**
- Scale: they need to process more competitive data than any other segment
- Integration: the value of ViralScopes multiplies when the data lives in their BI stack
- Access control: RBAC is a must-have for multi-team deployments

**Decision process:** Longer sales cycles (4–8 weeks). Involves IT and data engineering in the evaluation. Procurement process. Annual contracts preferred.

## 3. Revenue Model

### 3.1 Subscription Model (Primary)

**Structure:** Monthly or annual recurring subscription. Annual plans discounted by 20%.

**Plan tiers:**

| Plan | Monthly | Annual equiv. | Target segment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | £0 | £0 | Evaluation, solo hobbyists |
| Starter | £39 | £31/mo | Independent creators |
| Professional | £89 | £71/mo | Full-time creators, small teams |
| Business | £249 | £199/mo | Agencies, brand teams |
| Enterprise | Custom | Custom | Media companies, large agencies |

See [Pricing_Strategy.md](./Pricing_Strategy.md) for complete plan details and feature limits.

### 3.2 Freemium Strategy

The Free plan is a permanent acquisition mechanism, not a time-limited trial.

**Free plan purpose:**
- Demonstrates core value (viral scoring, trending feed) with real data
- Creates habit formation — users who check ViralScopes regularly are more likely to upgrade
- Generates word-of-mouth among creators who share ViralScopes screenshots and exports
- Lowers the acquisition barrier for creators who are revenue-constrained

**Free plan limits are set to:**
- Provide enough value to create genuine utility (20 videos/month, 1 watchlist)
- Create natural upgrade pressure as users want more volume, more watchlists, more export formats, and alert delivery to Discord/Slack

**Free-to-paid target:** 5% conversion within 30 days.

### 3.3 Enterprise Licensing

Enterprise customers are quoted individually based on:
- Seat count (minimum 10 seats)
- Monthly video analysis volume
- Number of workspaces
- Data retention period requirements
- SLA requirements and dedicated support

Enterprise contracts are annual, invoiced quarterly in advance, and include:
- A 30-day onboarding programme with dedicated CSM
- Custom data retention and DPA
- SLA with uptime guarantee and response time commitments
- Priority support channel (Slack Connect or dedicated email)

### 3.4 Usage Overages (Post-MVP)

Rather than hard-blocking users who exceed their plan limits, higher-tier users will have the option to pay per-unit overages:

| Resource | Overage rate |
|---|---|
| Video analyses | £0.05 per video above plan limit |
| API requests | £0.002 per 1,000 requests above plan limit |
| Exports | £0.50 per export above plan limit |

Overages are billed at the end of the billing period and added to the next invoice.

### 3.5 API Monetisation (v2.0)

When the Public API launches (v2.0), API access becomes a revenue stream in two ways:

1. **Included in plans:** Professional+ includes API access with rate limits per plan
2. **Pay-as-you-go tier:** Developers who don't need a full subscription can purchase API credits directly

| API product | Price | Target |
|---|---|---|
| API credits (PAYG) | £0.005 per API request | Developers, light integrators |
| Developer plan | £49/month (2,500 requests/day) | Regular integrators |
| Included in Pro+ | Rate-limited per plan | Full platform customers |

### 3.6 Marketplace Opportunities (v3.0)

The plugin marketplace creates a new revenue stream:
- **Revenue share:** 30% of plugin sales go to ViralScopes; 70% to the plugin developer
- **Featured placement:** Paid slot for promoted plugins (auction-based)

### 3.7 Affiliate & Referral Revenue

The affiliate programme (v2.0) drives customer acquisition at near-zero marginal cost:
- Referring customers earn 20% recurring commission for the lifetime of the referred customer's subscription
- Referred customers receive 10% off their first 3 months
- Affiliate payouts via bank transfer or PayPal, monthly

## 4. Acquisition Strategy

### 4.1 Product-Led Growth (PLG) — Primary

ViralScopes is built for product-led growth: the product itself is the primary acquisition engine.

**PLG mechanisms:**
- **Free tier:** Removes the barrier to trial; users experience value before paying
- **Viral score sharing:** Users share viral score screenshots and exports on Twitter/X and in creator communities — each share is organic marketing
- **"Powered by ViralScopes" on exports:** Exported PDFs and CSVs include a subtle attribution link (optional for Pro+)
- **Referral programme (v2.0):** Users earn commission on paid referrals

### 4.2 Content Marketing (SEO)

Content marketing is the highest-ROI acquisition channel for creator tools.

**Strategy:**
- **YouTube channel:** 2 long-form videos per month targeting creator education keywords ("how to find trending YouTube topics", "why this video went viral", "YouTube competitor research")
- **Blog:** 4 long-form posts per month targeting search queries creators use when researching tools
- **Free tools:** Simple free tools (e.g. a basic YouTube video analyser embedded on the website) drive organic traffic and email capture
- **Case studies:** Success stories from creators who used ViralScopes insights to grow their channel

**Target keywords (examples):**
- "how to find trending youtube topics" (3,600 searches/month)
- "youtube competitor analysis tool" (1,900 searches/month)
- "why did my youtube video go viral" (1,200 searches/month)
- "youtube viral score calculator" (500 searches/month)

### 4.3 Creator Community

Active participation in the communities where target customers spend time:

- **Twitter/X:** Share weekly "Why did this video go viral?" analyses using ViralScopes data
- **Reddit:** r/NewTubers, r/YouTube — provide genuine value, no spam
- **Discord:** Creator-focused Discord servers (Think Media, YouTube Creators Hub)
- **Facebook Groups:** Creator education Facebook groups (large, particularly for mid-tier creators)

**Community strategy:**
- Be genuinely helpful first; promotional second
- Share ViralScopes analyses as insights, not advertisements
- Build relationships with moderators and influential community members

### 4.4 Influencer & Creator Partnerships

**Strategy:** Partner with creator educators — YouTubers and podcasters who teach other creators how to grow.

- Identify 10–15 creator educators with audiences of 50k–500k
- Offer free Professional access in exchange for honest reviews and tutorials
- Co-create content: "I analysed 1,000 viral videos using ViralScopes — here's what I found"
- Affiliate commission structure for creator educator partners: 30% recurring

### 4.5 Product Hunt Launch

A coordinated Product Hunt launch targets the:
- Developer and startup community (early adopters, tech-savvy creators)
- Product Hunt newsletter (350k+ subscribers)
- "Product of the Day" placement if successful — historically generates 500–2,000 signups

**Launch preparation checklist:**
- [ ] Build a waitlist of 500+ subscribers before launch day
- [ ] Prepare launch assets (demo video, screenshots, copy)
- [ ] Brief hunter network to upvote and comment on launch day
- [ ] Prepare to respond personally to every comment within 24 hours

### 4.6 Paid Acquisition

Paid acquisition is tested conservatively and scaled only when unit economics are proven.

| Channel | Use case | CAC target | Budget (Month 3–6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search Ads | Target creator tool keywords | < £80 | £500/month |
| YouTube Ads (pre-roll) | Creator education content | < £100 | £500/month |
| Twitter/X Promoted | Creator community targeting | < £70 | £300/month |

Paid spend is paused if CAC exceeds the target for 2 consecutive months.

### 4.7 Partnerships

**YouTube ecosystem partners:**
- Video editing software (DaVinci Resolve community, Adobe Premiere users)
- Creator coaching programmes (offer ViralScopes as a tool recommendation)
- Talent agencies (white-label or referral arrangements)

**Agency partners:**
- Co-market with complementary SaaS tools used by agencies (project management, client reporting)
- Agency partner programme: agencies get 25% off Business plan + co-marketing support in exchange for case studies

## 5. Retention Strategy

### 5.1 Onboarding

The onboarding experience is the highest-leverage retention investment.

**Onboarding goal:** Deliver a genuine "aha moment" within the first session.

**Onboarding flow:**
1. **Create org** (30 seconds)
2. **Choose plan** (30 seconds)
3. **Set first watchlist** (60 seconds) — this is the key step; a watchlist means the user gets personalised data immediately
4. **Product tour** (2 minutes) — guided tooltips through the key dashboard sections
5. **First meaningful data** — the trending feed immediately shows videos in the user's area of interest

**Day 1 email:** Sends automatically 2 hours after signup; shows the user's first discovered trending video and its Viral Score breakdown.

**Day 3 email:** If the user has not logged in again, a "Did you know?" email showing a specific insight from their watchlist.

**Day 7 email:** If the user has not converted, a case study showing how another creator used ViralScopes.

### 5.2 Activation

**Activation definition:** A user is "activated" when they have:
- Created at least 1 watchlist
- Viewed at least 3 video detail pages
- Set at least 1 alert rule

This typically correlates with users who stay past 30 days. The onboarding flow and Day 1 email are designed to drive users to these activation milestones within the first session.

**Activation rate target:** 40% of new signups within 7 days.

### 5.3 Engagement

**Habit formation is the engine of retention.** ViralScopes aims to become a Monday morning habit for creators.

**Engagement mechanisms:**
- **Weekly alert digest** (every Monday 08:00 in user's timezone) — summarises the most significant alerts from the past 7 days even if no individual alert threshold was crossed
- **Changelog badge** — "New features this week" badge in the sidebar drives feature discovery
- **Personalised recommendations** — video recommendations are shown based on the user's watchlist topics, not a generic feed
- **Progress indicators** — "You've analysed 180/200 videos this month" creates gentle urgency near plan limits (and drives upgrades)

### 5.4 Feature Adoption

Each major feature has an in-product prompt to try it:

| Feature | Adoption prompt |
|---|---|
| Watchlists | Day 1 onboarding step |
| Alert rules | Tooltip on first visit to Watchlists page: "Set an alert for this watchlist" |
| Export | After user views 5+ video details: "Export these insights as a report" |
| API keys | When a Business/Pro user visits Settings for the 3rd time |
| Recommendations | After viewing a video detail: "See original content ideas for this video" |

### 5.5 Renewal Strategy

**30 days before renewal:**
- Email: "Your ViralScopes subscription renews in 30 days — here's what you've discovered this year"
- Include a personalised usage summary: videos analysed, top viral score found, alerts triggered, exports created

**Annual plan push:**
- At month 3–4, users who are on monthly plans receive an in-app prompt: "Switch to annual and save £95/year"
- Annual plan take-rate target: 25% of paying customers

**At-risk users (low engagement):**
- Users who haven't logged in for 14 days receive a re-engagement email
- Users who haven't logged in for 28 days receive a "We miss you" email with a specific insight from their watchlist
- Customer success proactively reaches out to Business customers who show < 30% feature adoption after 60 days

### 5.6 Community Building

A creator community around ViralScopes extends retention beyond product features:

- **Discord server (Month 6+):** ViralScopes community where creators share wins, ask questions, and discuss viral content trends
- **Creator showcase:** Monthly "ViralScopes success story" featured in the community and on social media
- **Weekly insights newsletter (Month 6+):** "This week in viral content" — trends, notable scores, and original analysis

## 6. Growth Strategy

### 6.1 Geographic Expansion

**Phase 1 (MVP):** English-language markets — UK, USA, Canada, Australia, Ireland.

**Phase 2 (v2.0, Month 18):** Spanish-speaking markets — Spain, Mexico, Latin America. Portuguese (Brazil). The Spanish and Portuguese YouTube creator economies are among the largest and fastest-growing globally.

**Phase 3 (v3.0+):** German, French, Hindi markets. Multi-language content analysis support required before entering these markets.

**Localisation strategy:**
- Pricing in local currency (via Paddle merchant of record)
- Local tax compliance handled by Paddle
- Content marketing in target language
- Interface translation via `next-intl` (architecture already in place)

### 6.2 Platform Expansion

**Platform expansion is the biggest growth lever beyond the initial English YouTube market.**

| Platform | Target | TAM expansion |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok (v2.0) | Month 18 | ~50M short-form creators globally |
| Instagram Reels (v2.0) | Month 20 | Existing YouTube creators who also post Reels |
| YouTube Shorts (v2.0) | Month 22 | Already within the YouTube API ecosystem |
| LinkedIn Video (v3.0) | Month 30 | High-value B2B creator segment |
| Podcasts (v3.0) | Month 36 | RSS-based; different analysis model |

### 6.3 Product Expansion

New features that expand usage within existing customer organisations:

- **AI Chat Interface (v1.5):** Drives daily active usage as creators ask ad hoc questions
- **Scheduled Reports (v1.5):** Increases stickiness for agency users; adds weekly touchpoints
- **Team Collaboration (v2.0):** Expands seat count within existing Business/Enterprise organisations
- **Public API (v2.0):** Embeds ViralScopes data into customer workflows, making cancellation costly
- **Chrome Extension (v1.5):** Creates a persistent touchpoint in the creator's daily YouTube browsing

### 6.4 Enterprise Sales

From Month 12, a dedicated enterprise sales motion begins:

**Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) for enterprise outbound:**
- Digital publisher or media company
- YouTube presence with 100k+ subscribers
- Team of 10+ people touching content
- Already using some form of competitive intelligence tool (indicates willingness to pay)

**Enterprise sales process:**
1. Outbound prospecting (LinkedIn Sales Navigator)
2. Personalised outreach with a custom Viral Score analysis of their channel
3. Discovery call (30 minutes)
4. Demo tailored to their specific use case
5. Pilot period (30 days, limited seats)
6. Proposal and contract
7. Onboarding and CSM assignment

**Target:** 2–3 enterprise contracts per quarter from Month 12 onwards.

### 7.5 Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)

```
LTV = ARPU × Gross Margin % × (1 / Monthly Churn Rate)

At steady state (Month 18+):
LTV = £52 × 76% × (1 / 0.05) = £52 × 0.76 × 20 = £790
```

| Stage | ARPU | Gross Margin | Churn | LTV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Month 6 | £52 | 71% | 7% | £527 |
| Month 12 | £52 | 74% | 6% | £641 |
| Month 18 | £52 | 76% | 5% | £790 |
| Month 24 | £52 | 77% | 4% | £1,001 |

### 7.6 LTV:CAC Ratio

| Stage | LTV | CAC | LTV:CAC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Month 6 | £527 | £50 | 10.5:1 ✅ |
| Month 12 | £641 | £50 | 12.8:1 ✅ |
| Month 18 | £790 | £60 | 13.2:1 ✅ |
| Month 24 | £1,001 | £60 | 16.7:1 ✅ |

*All scenarios comfortably exceed the 3:1 minimum and 5:1 target.*

### 7.7 Payback Period

```
CAC Payback = CAC / (ARPU × Gross Margin %)
            = £50 / (£52 × 0.74)
            = £50 / £38.48
            = 1.3 months
```

**Payback period: ~6 weeks.** Extremely short — common in high-margin SaaS products with content-driven acquisition.

## 8. Risks

### 8.1 Market Risks

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creator economy contraction | Low | High | Diversify to agencies and brands (less consumer-cycle sensitive) |
| YouTube algorithm change reduces creator viability | Low | High | Multi-platform strategy (TikTok, Instagram) already in roadmap |
| Market saturation by a well-funded competitor | Medium | High | Speed to market advantage; proprietary data moat; ethical positioning |
| Creators unwilling to pay for tools | Low | High | Comparable tools (VidIQ, TubeBuddy) already prove willingness to pay |

### 8.2 Competitive Risks

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| VidIQ or TubeBuddy adds AI analysis | High | Medium | Proprietary Viral Score algorithm and structural analysis are defensible; build the moat deeper |
| YouTube launches a native analytics tool | Low | High | Diversify to multi-platform; YouTube won't share competitor intelligence |
| OpenAI or Anthropic launches a content analysis product | Medium | Medium | We are a workflow + data product, not just an AI wrapper; proprietary data is the moat |
| Well-funded startup clones the product | Medium | High | Speed, data flywheel, brand trust, and customer relationships are the defences |

### 8.3 Operational Risks

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube API quota exhausted at scale | High | High | Quota manager + RapidAPI/Apify fallback + per-plan quota allocation |
| AI API cost explosion | High | Medium | Aggressive caching + tiered analysis + model selection |
| Key engineer departure | Medium | High | Documentation standards; pair programming; no single person owns a critical system |
| n8n instability at high volume | Medium | Medium | Dead-letter queue; idempotent workflows; horizontal scaling at Stage 2 |

### 8.4 Financial Risks

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free-to-paid conversion < 3% | Medium | High | Improve onboarding; test pricing; add more free→paid value bridges |
| Monthly churn > 10% sustained | Medium | High | Invest in customer success; improve product stickiness; annual plan incentives |
| CAC exceeds £200 (paid channels don't work) | Medium | Medium | Lean into organic/content; referral programme; reduce paid spend |
| Year 1 cash burn exceeds projection | Medium | Medium | Conservative hiring; extend runway via annual plan cash flow |

### 8.5 Technical Risks

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube API terms change restricting data use | Low | High | Multi-source strategy; terms-compliant data use |
| AI model deprecation | Medium | Medium | Dual provider (Claude + OpenAI); abstraction layer |
| Security breach / data exposure | Low | Critical | Defence-in-depth security; RLS; audit logging; incident response plan |
| GDPR enforcement action | Low | High | GDPR compliance built into MVP; DPA for Enterprise; right to deletion |
