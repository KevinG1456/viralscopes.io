## 1. Market Positioning

### 1.1 Target Market

**Primary market:** The professional creator economy — individuals and teams who manage YouTube content as their primary or significant professional activity.

**Market size:**
- YouTube has 800 million+ videos and 50+ million content creators globally
- The creator economy is estimated at $250B+ globally (2026)
- Creators spending £39–£249/month on tools is well-established (TubeBuddy and VidIQ each have hundreds of thousands of paying subscribers)
- The addressable market for ViralScopes at the Professional and Business tiers is estimated at 8–15 million creators and teams globally

**Initial focus:**
- English-language content and English-speaking markets (UK, USA, Canada, Australia, Ireland)
- Creators with 10,000–10,000,000 subscribers (serious but not yet enterprise-scale)
- Agencies and brand teams managing YouTube channels professionally

**Market timing:**
Three forces converge to make 2026 the right moment:
1. AI models capable of nuanced content structural analysis are commercially available at viable cost
2. The creator economy is professionalising — creators are increasingly willing to invest in tools
3. No direct competitor has assembled deep AI structural analysis, viral scoring, and ethical recommendation generation in one product

### 1.2 Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | Segment | Strength | Weakness | ViralScopes advantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **VidIQ** | Individual creators | Large user base; established brand | Keyword-focused; no structural AI analysis; no viral scoring | Depth of analysis; ethical positioning; Viral Score |
| **TubeBuddy** | Individual creators | Browser extension UX; strong SEO tools | No AI content analysis; limited competitor intelligence | Standalone platform; deeper analysis; multi-channel alerts |
| **Social Blade** | All | Historical statistics | Data only; no guidance; no AI | Actionable recommendations; forward-looking trend detection |
| **Exploding Topics** | Marketers | Trend discovery | Not YouTube-specific; no content analysis | YouTube-native; structural analysis; viral scoring |
| **Semrush (YouTube)** | Marketing teams | Broad platform | YouTube is an afterthought; no content structure | Purpose-built; creator-native language; hook/title analysis |
| **Tubular Labs** | Enterprise | Enterprise-grade data | Expensive; complex; no AI recommendations | Accessible pricing; actionable output; ethical AI |
| **Manual research** | All | Free; flexible | Time-intensive; inconsistent; no synthesis | Automates 95% of manual research time |

**Competitive conclusion:** No existing product combines large-scale YouTube data collection, multi-stream AI structural analysis, proprietary viral scoring, trend detection with velocity, and ethically-generated original recommendations. This is the gap ViralScopes occupies.

### 1.3 Unique Selling Propositions

| USP | Proof point | Competitor parity? |
|---|---|---|
| **Proprietary Viral Score (0–100)** | 9-signal weighted algorithm; explainable; confidence-rated | No competitor has an equivalent |
| **Structural pattern analysis** | Detects hook types, title formulas, narrative arcs — not just views | No competitor does this |
| **Ethical AI framework** | System prompt explicitly prohibits reproducing creator work; labelled in UI | No competitor has this positioning |
| **Trend velocity classification** | Emerging / evergreen / declining — not just "trending" | No competitor classifies this way |
| **Opportunity Engine** | Demand × growth ÷ competition ranking of untapped niches | No equivalent product feature |
| **Dead-letter + admin observability** | Engineering-grade reliability with transparent failure recovery | Not relevant to end users; differentiates enterprise trust |
| **Multi-channel alert dispatch** | Email, Discord, Slack, Telegram, webhook — all in one | Competitors offer fewer channels |

### 1.4 Positioning Statement

**For** content creators, agencies, and media teams **who** need to understand why content goes viral and create original content that performs,

**ViralScopes.io** is the AI-powered content intelligence platform

**that** analyses the structural patterns behind viral YouTube performance and delivers original, ethically generated creative guidance.

**Unlike** VidIQ, TubeBuddy, or manual research,

**ViralScopes** provides deep structural analysis (hook types, title formulas, narrative arcs), a proprietary Viral Score with explainable components, and an ethical AI framework that generates original recommendations instead of facilitating imitation.

## 2. Customer Segments

### 2.1 Solo Creators

**Profile:** Independent YouTubers managing their own channel as a primary or significant income source. Subscriber range: 10,000–500,000. Operating solo or with 1–2 contractors.

**Needs:**
- Understand which structural decisions drove their best-performing videos
- Discover emerging topics 2–4 weeks before they peak
- Replace hours of manual competitor research with automated intelligence
- Get original content ideas grounded in real performance data

**Pain points:**
- 5–10 hours per week of manual research with no systematic methodology
- Trend awareness is reactive — content produced about topics after peak
- Cannot quantify why some videos succeed and others fail
- Limited budget for multiple tools; need a single source of content intelligence

**Buying motivations:**
- Time: "This gives me back my Sunday evenings"
- Competitive edge: "I'll see trends before others in my niche"
- Confidence: "I can make data-backed content decisions"
- Revenue: "Better-performing content means more ad and sponsorship revenue"

**Expected Lifetime Value (LTV):**
- Plan: Starter (£39/month) or Professional (£89/month)
- Average: £52/month × 20-month average lifetime = **£1,040**
- With annual plan (20% discount, lower churn): **~£1,400**

---

### 2.2 Agencies

**Profile:** Content strategy agencies managing 3–20 creator clients. Teams of 3–25 people. Revenue model: monthly retainer per creator client (typically £500–£3,000/month per client).

**Needs:**
- Deliver weekly content strategy briefs for all clients efficiently
- Competitive intelligence per client's niche
- Objective data to justify strategy recommendations to clients
- Scale strategy delivery without proportional headcount growth

**Pain points:**
- Each client brief requires 2–3 hours of manual research
- Strategy recommendations are based on team intuition, not data
- No systematic way to set up competitive monitoring per client
- Time spent on research is non-billable overhead eating into margins

**Buying motivations:**
- Efficiency: "We can serve 3× as many clients with the same team"
- Differentiation: "Our briefs are data-backed; competitors' are not"
- Client retention: "Clients stay longer when they see measurable results"
- Margin: "We convert non-billable research hours into billable output time"

**Expected LTV:**
- Plan: Business (£249/month) with 5 workspaces
- Average lifetime: 28 months (higher than individual creators due to stickiness)
- LTV: £249 × 28 = **£6,972**
- With annual plan: **~£8,700**

---

### 2.3 Marketing Teams (Brand/In-House)

**Profile:** In-house content teams at consumer brands managing a YouTube channel as part of their marketing strategy. Teams of 2–8 people. Budget comes from marketing department.

**Needs:**
- Benchmark own channel against 3–10 competitor channels
- Build a data-backed content calendar
- Justify content investment decisions to CMO or senior leadership
- Monitor when competitors publish high-performing content

**Pain points:**
- YouTube Analytics only shows internal data — no competitive benchmarking
- Content calendar decisions based on instinct or trend reports from the marketing team
- No objective way to measure content quality relative to the competitive landscape
- Reactive to competitor content rather than proactive

**Buying motivations:**
- Accountability: "We can show leadership data that justifies our budget"
- Competitive awareness: "We know what our competitors are doing"
- Efficiency: "We stop producing content about topics that have already peaked"
- Quality: "Our content strategy is systematically grounded in what works"

**Expected LTV:**
- Plan: Professional (£89/month) or Business (£249/month)
- Average: £130/month × 22 months = **£2,860**

### 2.4 Startups

**Profile:** Early-stage companies using YouTube as a primary growth or marketing channel. Small teams (2–15 people) where the founder or a generalist marketer manages content.

**Needs:**
- Understand what content formats work in their category
- Get original video ideas without a dedicated content strategist
- Monitor what well-funded competitors in their space are doing
- Maximise impact from limited content production resources

**Pain points:**
- No budget for a dedicated content strategist
- Limited time — founder or marketer is doing content as one of many responsibilities
- No systematic approach to content strategy in a competitive market

**Buying motivations:**
- Efficiency at low cost: "One tool that replaces a content strategist for early-stage"
- Competitive intelligence: "See what well-funded competitors are making that works"

**Expected LTV:**
- Plan: Starter (£39) or Professional (£89)
- Average: £60/month × 14 months (higher churn due to startup volatility) = **£840**

### 2.5 SMBs

**Profile:** Small and medium businesses (10–250 employees) where YouTube is a content marketing and brand-building channel. Managed by a marketing team of 1–5 people.

**Needs:**
- Similar to brand marketing teams but with tighter budgets
- Value data-backed decisions due to limited resources
- Often managing YouTube alongside multiple other marketing channels

**Buying motivations:**
- Clear ROI: "This directly improves our video performance, which drives leads/sales"
- Simplicity: "One tool that does competitive research and suggests content ideas"

**Expected LTV:**
- Plan: Professional (£89/month) or Business (£249/month)
- Average: £120/month × 18 months = **£2,160**

---

### 2.6 Enterprises

**Profile:** Large media companies, publishers, multi-channel networks, major consumer brands with significant YouTube presence. Teams of 10–100+ people touching YouTube content. Annual content budgets of £500k+.

**Needs:**
- Systematic competitive monitoring at scale (50+ competitor channels)
- Team-level access control (different workspaces per editorial team)
- API integration with internal data infrastructure and BI tools
- SLA and dedicated support commitments
- Enterprise procurement compliance (SSO, DPA, security review)

**Pain points:**
- No existing tool handles the scale of competitive monitoring required
- Current tools don't offer granular RBAC for multi-team deployments
- Cannot integrate YouTube intelligence data into existing BI stacks
- Manual processes are inconsistent across a large editorial team

**Buying motivations:**
- Scale: The only tool that can handle their monitoring volume
- Integration: Data feeds directly into their internal tooling
- Trust: SLA, dedicated CSM, and security compliance are requirements

**Expected LTV:**
- Plan: Enterprise (£1,500–£3,000/month average)
- Average: £2,000/month × 36 months (long contracts, very low churn) = **£72,000**
- With multi-year contracts: **£90,000+**

## 3. Go-to-Market Strategy

### 3.1 GTM Phases

**Phase 1 — Seed (Months 1–3):** Build the foundation; target the most accessible segment (individual creators) through content and community.

**Phase 2 — Grow (Months 4–12):** Expand creator base; begin targeting agencies; launch referral programme.

**Phase 3 — Scale (Months 12–24):** Enterprise motion begins; multi-platform expansion; partner ecosystem.

### 3.2 Product-Led Growth (PLG)

PLG is the primary acquisition engine. The product sells itself to users who can immediately experience its value.

**PLG mechanisms:**

| Mechanism | Description | Expected impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Free tier** | Permanent free plan creates zero-friction trial | Volume of signups; top of funnel |
| **Viral content sharing** | Users share Viral Score screenshots on social media | Organic brand awareness in creator communities |
| **"Analysed by ViralScopes"** | Optional attribution on exported PDFs | Organic marketing from every export |
| **Early aha moment** | Discovery workflow delivers value in < 60 seconds of first login | Drives Day 1 activation and retention |
| **Referral programme (Month 12)** | Users earn 20% recurring commission on paid referrals | 15–20% of new customers by Month 18 |

### 3.3 SEO & Content Marketing

Content marketing is the highest ROI acquisition channel for a creator tool — creators are the most SEO-active professional audience.

**SEO targets:**

| Keyword | Monthly searches (est.) | Difficulty | Intent |
|---|---|---|---|
| "youtube competitor analysis tool" | 2,400 | Medium | Commercial |
| "how to find trending youtube topics" | 4,800 | Medium | Informational |
| "why did my youtube video go viral" | 1,900 | Low | Informational |
| "youtube viral score" | 800 | Low | Commercial |
| "youtube content intelligence" | 600 | Low | Commercial |

| "youtube hook type analysis" | 400 | Low | Commercial |
| "vidiq alternative" | 1,600 | High | Commercial |
| "tubebuddy alternative" | 2,100 | High | Commercial |

**Content types:**

| Content type | Frequency | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Long-form blog posts (1,500–3,000 words) | 4/month | SEO ranking for informational keywords |
| "Why did this video go viral?" analysis | 2/month | Social sharing + brand authority |

| YouTube tutorials (own channel) | 2/month | Awareness in creator community + YouTube SEO |

**Email capture strategy:**
- Free weekly "Viral Trends" newsletter (trending topics + top Viral Score of the week)

### 3.5 Influencer Partnerships

**Tier 1 — Creator Educators (audiences of 100k–2M):**
- Target: 5–8 high-trust creator education channels (think media, how-to-YouTube channels)
- Offer: Free lifetime Professional access + 30% affiliate commission
- Ask: Honest review video, tutorial, or "tool I use" mention
- Expected: 100–500 signups per partner per mention

**Tier 2 — Mid-tier creators (audiences of 10k–100k in target niches):**
- Target: 20–30 creators in finance, fitness, tech, and lifestyle niches
- Offer: Free 6-month Professional access
- Ask: Natural product mentions in relevant videos
- Expected: 20–100 signups per partner

**Tier 3 — Micro-influencers (audiences of 1k–10k in creator community):**
- Target: 50+ active community members and moderators
- Offer: Free Starter access perpetually
- Ask: Community advocacy, forum recommendations
- Expected: 5–30 signups per partner (volume play)

### 3.6 Referral Programme (Month 12)

**Structure:**
- Referrer earns: 20% recurring monthly commission on referred customer's subscription for as long as they remain a customer
- Referred customer receives: 10% off first 3 months
- Minimum payout: £20 (PayPal or bank transfer, monthly)
- Tracking: Unique referral link per customer; cookie duration 30 days
- Dashboard: In-product referral dashboard showing clicks, conversions, earnings

**Target:** 15% of new paying customers from referral by Month 18.

### 3.7 Affiliate Programme

**Distinction from referral:** The affiliate programme targets content publishers, bloggers, and comparison sites — not existing customers.

**Structure:**
- Commission: 25% recurring on monthly plans; 15% on annual plans
- Cookie duration: 60 days
- Minimum payout: £50
- Programme platform: Rewardful or PartnerStack

**Affiliate targets:**
- "Best YouTube tools" listicle sites and review blogs
- Creator economy newsletters
- Marketing software comparison sites (Capterra, G2, Product Hunt alternatives)
- Creator-focused podcast show notes

### 3.8 Paid Advertising

Paid advertising is deployed conservatively — only after organic channels are established and CAC benchmarks are validated.

**Month 1–3:** No paid advertising. All budget goes to content creation.

**Month 4–6 (Test phase, £1,500/month total):**

| Channel | Budget | Target | Metric to watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search (brand + competitor) | £500/month | "VidIQ alternative", "TubeBuddy alternative" | CAC < £80 |
| YouTube pre-roll | £500/month | Creator education content viewers | CAC < £100 |
| Twitter/X promoted posts | £500/month | Creator and marketer audiences | CAC < £70 |

**Month 7–12 (Scale phase, up to £5,000/month if CAC targets met):**
- Scale winning channels; pause underperformers
- Add retargeting campaigns for website visitors who did not convert
- Test LinkedIn ads for agency and enterprise segments

### 3.9 Community Strategy

Community is a long-term retention and acquisition play:

**ViralScopes Discord Server (Month 6):**
- Channels: #viral-finds, #trend-alerts, #creator-wins, #product-feedback, #general
- Weekly "Viral Score Challenge" — members share a video; ViralScopes analyses it live
- Monthly "Trend Report" thread with community contributions
- Target: 1,000 active members by Month 12

**Creator Showcase Programme (Month 4):**
- Monthly feature: "Creator who used ViralScopes insights to grow"
- Shared on all social channels with the creator's attribution
- Incentive for creators to share their results publicly

**Weekly Insights Newsletter (Month 6):**
- "This Week in Viral Content" — top trends, biggest viral scores, emerging niches
- Target: 20,000 subscribers by Month 18
- Monetisation potential at scale: sponsorships from complementary creator tools

## 4. Sales Strategy

### 4.1 Self-Service Sales (Free → Starter → Professional)

The majority of revenue in Year 1 and Year 2 comes from self-service conversions.

**Self-service sales funnel:**

```
Website visit
    │
    ▼
Free sign-up (no credit card)
    │
    ▼
Activation: set watchlist + view 3 video analyses + set 1 alert
    │
    ▼ (within 7 days)
In-product upgrade prompt: "You're using 85% of your monthly video quota"
    │
    ▼
Stripe Checkout → Starter or Professional plan
    │
    ▼ (within 6 months)
Business plan upgrade: "Add a team member" or "Need more workspaces"
```

**Conversion optimisation tactics:**
- Progress bars showing quota consumption create natural urgency
- "You're getting close to your limit" emails at 80% of monthly quota
- Upgrade comparison modal: shows the exact feature that would unblock them on the next plan
- Annual plan prompt at Month 3 of monthly subscription: "Save £214/year by switching to annual"

### 4.2 Inside Sales (Business Plan)

From Month 9 onwards, an inside sales motion targets agency and brand team prospects.

**Target:** Companies showing intent signals:
- Signed up for free but not converted after 14 days (email outreach)
- Company domain suggests agency or brand (vs individual creator email)
- High session count but not upgraded (engaged but not converting)

**Inside sales approach:**
- Personalised video loom (2–3 minutes) showing a ViralScopes analysis of their own channel or a competitor's channel
- No generic sales emails — every outreach includes a specific insight relevant to them
- 3-touch sequence: personalised video → follow-up with relevant trend report → final value-add
- Target: 15% reply rate, 30% of replies convert to demo call

**Demo call structure (30 minutes):**
1. Understand their current research process (10 min)
2. Show the product using their actual niche/competitors as examples (15 min)
3. Address questions + pricing + next steps (5 min)

### 4.3 Enterprise Sales (Month 12+)

Enterprise sales require a dedicated sales motion — self-service is insufficient for organisations needing SSO, DPA, custom contracts, and procurement approval.

**Enterprise Ideal Customer Profile (ICP):**
- Digital publisher or media company
- 100,000+ YouTube subscribers on at least one channel
- Team of 10+ people managing content
- Already paying for some form of competitive intelligence tool
- UK, US, CA, or AU based (English-language content focus)

**Enterprise sales process:**

| Stage | Duration | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| Prospecting | Ongoing | LinkedIn Sales Navigator; inbound from content; events |
| Outreach | 1–2 weeks | Personalised video + channel analysis; 3-touch sequence |
| Discovery call | 30 min | Understand scale, team, current tools, decision criteria |
| Demo | 60 min | Tailored to their specific channels, competitors, and workflows |
| Pilot | 30 days | Business plan access for 5 users; CSM-guided |
| Proposal | 1 week | Custom pricing; SLA tier; contract terms |
| Negotiation | 1–2 weeks | Legal and procurement review |
| Close | 1 week | Contract signature; onboarding kickoff |

**Target enterprise close rate:** 25% of qualified pipeline (discovery call held)
**Average enterprise sales cycle:** 8–12 weeks

### 4.4 Partner Sales

From Month 18, a partner programme expands distribution through complementary tools and services.

**Partner types:**

| Partner type | Description | Revenue model |
|---|---|---|
| **Technology partners** | Integrations with complementary tools (Notion, Airtable, Slack) | Revenue share on upgrade attributions |
| **Agency partners** | Creator agencies who resell ViralScopes to their clients | 25% commission on client subscriptions |
| **Creator educator partners** | Courses and communities that teach creators | Affiliate commission + co-marketing |
| **Platform partners** | YouTube-adjacent platforms (editing tools, scheduling tools) | Cross-promotion and bundled offers |

## 5. Customer Success

### 5.1 Onboarding

The onboarding experience is the single highest-leverage investment for retention.

**Onboarding design principles:**
- Time to first value: < 60 seconds from account creation
- The "aha moment" happens in the first session (seeing a real Viral Score on a real video in their niche)
- Every onboarding step has a clear completion trigger and progress indicator

**Self-serve onboarding (Free, Starter, Professional):**

| Step | Duration | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Create org | 30 seconds | Account structure in place |
| 2. Choose plan | 30 seconds | Commitment to the platform |
| 3. Set first watchlist | 60 seconds | Personalised data immediately |
| 4. Product tour | 2 minutes | Understand where value lives |
| **Total** | **~4 minutes** | User is set up and has seen real data |

**Automated onboarding email sequence:**

| Email | Timing | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome | Immediately on signup | What ViralScopes does; link to finish setup |
| Day 1 | 2 hours after signup | "Here's what we found in your niche this week" — personalised insight |
| Day 3 | If not logged in again | "You might have missed this" — a specific trending video in their watchlist topic |
| Day 7 | If not converted | Creator success story; comparison with free plan limits |
| Day 14 | If not converted | Last outreach; offer to book a 1:1 setup call (for high-intent users) |

**Business plan onboarding:**
- Dedicated CSM assigned within 24 hours of Business plan subscription
- 45-minute onboarding call: workspace setup, competitor watchlists for each client, alert configuration
- 30-day check-in: review usage; identify underused features; proactively address churn risk

**Enterprise onboarding:**
- 30-day structured programme with dedicated CSM
- Week 1: Technical setup (SSO, API integration, workspace structure)
- Week 2: Power user training (3× 60-minute sessions)
- Week 3–4: Rollout to full team; ongoing support
- Day 30: Business review: ROI analysis against baseline

### 5.2 Activation

**Activation definition:** A user is "activated" when they complete all three activation milestones:
- [ ] Created at least 1 watchlist
- [ ] Viewed at least 3 Video Detail pages
- [ ] Set at least 1 alert rule

**Why these three:** Together, they represent a user who has personalised the product (watchlist), understood its core output (video analysis), and integrated it into their workflow (alerts). Users who complete all three within 7 days have materially higher 30-day retention.

**Activation rate target:** 40% of new signups within 7 days.

**Activation tactics:**
- Onboarding steps directly guide users to these three milestones
- In-product checklist: "3 steps to get full value from ViralScopes" — visible on the Home dashboard for the first 14 days
- Each activation action triggers a success toast: "Great! You'll now get alerts when any tracked channel posts a high-scoring video."

### 5.3 Engagement

**Habit formation is the primary driver of retention.**

Target habit: Opening ViralScopes every Monday morning to review the week's trending content and plan the week's content strategy.

**Engagement mechanisms:**

| Mechanism | Description | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly alert digest email | Summary of the top alerts from the past 7 days | Every Monday 07:00 (user timezone) |
| "This week in your niche" push | 3 trending videos relevant to user's watchlists | Every Monday 08:00 |
| New feature badge | Sidebar badge when new features ship | Per release |
| Progress nudge | "You've used 85% of your monthly quota" | As triggered |
| Viral Score achievement | "The highest score in your niche this month was 94.2 — see why" | Monthly |

**DAU/MAU target:** > 30% (30% of monthly active users visit at least once per day)

### 5.4 Retention

**Churn signals and interventions:**

| Signal | Threshold | Intervention |
|---|---|---|
| No login | 14 days | Automated re-engagement email: "Here's what you missed" |
| No login | 28 days | Personal email from founder: "Is everything OK? Can I help?" |
| Low feature adoption | < 3 features used in 30 days | In-app prompt + email suggesting next feature |
| Downgrade intent | Clicks "Change plan" | Show value summary before downgrade; offer annual plan |
| Cancellation attempt | Clicks "Cancel" | Show usage stats + what they'll lose; offer 1-month pause option |

**Retention drivers by plan:**

| Plan | Primary retention driver |
|---|---|
| Starter | Alerts (creators depend on Discord/Slack notifications) |
| Professional | API integration (switching cost once in workflows) |
| Business | Multiple workspaces + team members (org-level stickiness) |
| Enterprise | API + SSO + DPA + dedicated CSM (very high switching cost) |

### 5.5 Expansion

Expansion (upselling and cross-selling) is the primary driver of NRR > 100%.

**Natural expansion triggers:**

| Trigger | User action | Expansion opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| Quota limit approached | 85% of monthly videos used | Upgrade to next plan |
| Watchlist limit reached | Tries to add 6th watchlist on Starter | Upgrade to Professional |
| Team invite blocked | Tries to add 2nd seat on Starter/Professional | Upgrade to next plan |
| Export format blocked | Wants PDF on Starter | Upgrade to Professional |
| API access requested | Asks about API via support | Upgrade to Professional |
| Multiple workspaces needed | Agency use case identified | Upgrade to Business |
| SSO requested | IT team asks about SSO | Upgrade to Enterprise |

**CSM-led expansion (Business and Enterprise):**
- Quarterly business reviews include an expansion conversation
- CSM tracks feature adoption and proactively recommends plan upgrades based on usage patterns
- "You have 3 out of 5 workspace slots used — here's what Business clients typically do with the remaining 2"

### 5.6 Upselling

**Upsell paths:**

```
Free → Starter:         "You've hit your 20 video limit"
Starter → Professional: "Add 2 more team members" or "Get API access"
Professional → Business: "Create separate workspaces for each client"
Business → Enterprise:  "Add SSO for your company" or "Need SLA guarantees?"
Monthly → Annual:       "Save £214/year — switch to annual before your next renewal"
```

### 5.7 Cross-Selling (Post-MVP)

Cross-selling opportunities when adjacent products launch:
- Chrome Extension (v1.5): Prompt Professional users to install during active browsing sessions
- AI Chat Interface (v1.5): Prompt Starter users as a Business/Pro feature
- Scheduled Reports (v1.5): Prompt Business users to set up automated weekly reports
- Public API (v2.0): Promote to all Professional+ users as a workflow integration

### 6.5 Unit Economics

| Metric | Definition | Target (Month 18) |
|---|---|---|
| **LTV** | ARPU × Gross Margin × (1 / Monthly Churn) | > £790 |
| **LTV:CAC** | LTV / CAC | > 10:1 |
| **Payback period** | CAC / (ARPU × Gross Margin) | < 6 weeks |
| **Gross margin** | (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue | > 75% |
| **EBITDA margin** | EBITDA / Revenue | > 0% by Month 20 |

### 6.6 Leading vs Lagging Indicators

| Indicator type | Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Leading** | Activation rate (7-day) | Predicts 30-day retention |
| **Leading** | DAU/MAU ratio | Predicts monthly churn |
| **Leading** | Feature adoption: alerts | Alerts-active users churn at half the rate of non-alerts users |
| **Leading** | NPS | Predicts referral and expansion revenue |
| **Lagging** | Monthly churn | Reports the past 30 days of retention |
| **Lagging** | MRR | Reports revenue already earned |
| **Lagging** | LTV | Requires historical data to compute accurately |

**Review cadence:**
- Daily: New signups, activation completions, MRR added
- Weekly: DAU/MAU, feature adoption, alert activations, support ticket volume
- Monthly: Full metric review — churn, NRR, NPS, CAC, LTV, ARPU
- Quarterly: Unit economics, LTV:CAC, cohort analysis, channel attribution
