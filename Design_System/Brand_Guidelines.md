## 1. Brand Foundation

### 1.1 Mission

> **To give every content creator access to the data-driven content strategy tools that were previously reserved for large media companies — without ever compromising the originality or integrity of their creative work.**

The mission has three non-negotiable components:
- **Access** — the platform must be priced and designed for working creators, not just enterprise budgets
- **Data-driven** — every insight must be grounded in real performance data, not intuition or trends
- **Without compromising originality** — the platform must never facilitate copying; it must inspire original work

### 1.2 Vision

> **ViralScopes.io becomes the standard intelligence infrastructure for professional content creation.**

In five years, every professional creator — from a solo YouTuber to a 50-person media company — uses ViralScopes the way a professional marketer uses Google Analytics: it is expected, assumed, and essential.

**What this means for the brand:**
- The brand must feel institutional enough to be trusted by enterprise teams
- The brand must feel accessible enough to resonate with individual creators
- The brand must feel intelligent enough to justify the "intelligence platform" positioning

### 1.3 Core Values

| Value | What it means | How it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| **Intelligence** | We process complexity so our users don't have to | Deep analysis with clear, simple outputs |
| **Integrity** | We never help users copy or imitate other creators | The ethical AI framework is a brand promise, not a legal disclaimer |
| **Precision** | Scores are numbers, not ranges; analysis is specific, not vague | 87.4 not "Very High"; "Question hook" not "engaging opening" |
| **Accessibility** | Powerful tools should be available to creators at every level | Freemium model; clear pricing; no jargon |
| **Originality** | We celebrate creativity; we just make it data-informed | Recommendations are original; the product inspires, not imitates |

### 1.4 Brand Promise

> **"We'll show you why it works. You make something better."**

This promise encapsulates the product's value in a single sentence:
- "We'll show you why it works" — the analytical half: Viral Scores, hook analysis, trend detection
- "You make something better" — the creative half: original recommendations, structural inspiration, not copying

The brand promise is used internally to test every product and marketing decision: *Does this help the user understand why content works, and does it help them create something better?*

### 1.5 Brand Positioning

ViralScopes occupies the intersection of **analytical rigour** and **creative empowerment** — a space no competitor currently owns.

```
                HIGH ANALYTICAL RIGOUR
                        │
                        │  ● ViralScopes
           Ethical      │       Creative
           quadrant     │       empowerment
─────────────────────────┼──────────────────────
     Low creative        │    High creative
     empowerment         │    empowerment
                        │
                        │
                LOW ANALYTICAL RIGOUR

  TubeBuddy ●  VidIQ ●  │  ● Social Blade
```

**Positioning statement:** ViralScopes is the only content intelligence platform that reveals the structural patterns behind viral performance and turns them into original, ethical creative guidance. Unlike keyword research tools that tell you what is popular, ViralScopes tells you *why* — and inspires original creation rather than imitation.

### 1.6 Brand Personality

ViralScopes speaks like a **brilliant, direct research analyst who also deeply respects creativity**. Not a bot. Not a marketer. Not an academic. A sharp, insightful colleague who has done the data work so you can focus on the creative work.

| Trait | Sounds like | Does not sound like |
|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | "This video scored 87 because the hook opens with a specific statistic — viewers who hear numbers in the first 5 seconds watch 40% longer on average." | "Great content gets great results!" |
| **Direct** | "The topic is declining. Post by Friday or wait for the next cycle." | "This topic may or may not be experiencing some changes in performance metrics." |
| **Trustworthy** | "Confidence: 0.91 — we're highly confident in this score because the video has 14 days of engagement data." | "Our AI thinks this might be a high scorer!" |
| **Enabling** | "Here's an original hook idea inspired by the statistic pattern: [specific suggestion]." | "Go viral with our proven formula!" |
| **Measured** | "Emerging trend — 38% velocity growth. Worth creating for now. Estimated peak: 2–4 weeks." | "🔥🔥🔥 THIS IS BLOWING UP RIGHT NOW!!!" |

### 1.7 Brand Voice and Tone

#### Voice (constant)
The brand voice is the consistent character of ViralScopes communication. It never changes regardless of context.

**ViralScopes always sounds:**
- Specific and precise (numbers, not generalities)
- Direct (short sentences, active voice)
- Knowledgeable (earned authority, not arrogant)
- Human (a person wrote this, not a content template)
- Ethical (the platform's values are visible in its language)

**ViralScopes never sounds:**
- Hype-driven ("go viral!", "explode your channel!", "secret formula")
- Vague ("great content", "amazing results", "significant growth")
- Corporate ("leverage synergies", "best-in-class", "solution")
- Dismissive of creators' creative work
- Condescending to less technical users

#### Tone (context-dependent)
The tone adapts to the context while the voice remains constant.

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Dashboard data | Neutral, precise | "Viral Score: 87.4 (confidence: 0.91)" |
| Trend alerts | Urgent but calm | "New: Cortisol morning routine — emerging fast. 38% velocity. Post this week." |
| Empty states | Encouraging | "No videos here yet. Add a watchlist to start discovering what's working in your niche." |
| Error messages | Direct, helpful | "That YouTube URL isn't valid. Try: youtube.com/watch?v=..." |
| Marketing copy | Confident, creator-native | "Know why your next video will work before you film it." |
| Onboarding | Warm, guided | "Let's find the first topic your audience is searching for right now." |
| Support emails | Empathetic, solution-focused | "I can see the export failed — here's what happened and how to fix it." |

## 2. Visual Identity

*Full visual specifications are in [Visual_Design_System.md](./Visual_Design_System.md). This section provides the essential reference.*

### 2.1 Logo

**Primary logo:** Icon mark (scope/crosshair symbol) + "ViralScopes" wordmark in Inter SemiBold, horizontal layout.

**Minimum sizes:**
- Full horizontal logo: 120px wide
- Icon mark alone: 24px

**Approved on:** Deep Space `#070B14`, dark surfaces, white `#FFFFFF`, brand blue `#1D8CF8` (campaigns only).

**Never:** Rotate, stretch, recolour, add effects, or use on busy backgrounds without contrast testing.

→ Full logo guidelines: [Visual_Design_System.md §12–14](./Visual_Design_System.md)

### 2.2 Brand Colours

| Token | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| **Electric Blue** | `#1D8CF8` | Primary actions, links, focus states, active elements |
| **Deep Space** | `#070B14` | Default background (dark theme) |
| **Violet Accent** | `#7C5CFC` | Secondary highlights, gradient accents, AI-related UI |
| **Success Green** | `#10B981` | Positive trends, success states, high viral scores (85–100) |
| **Warning Amber** | `#F59E0B` | Caution states, declining trends, quota warnings |
| **Error Red** | `#EF4444` | Errors, low viral scores (0–30), destructive actions |

→ Full palette: [Visual_Design_System.md §5–6](./Visual_Design_System.md)

### 2.3 Typography

**Primary typeface:** Inter (Google Fonts)
**Monospace:** JetBrains Mono (code, API keys, identifiers)
**Display headline size:** 48px, weight 700, letter-spacing −0.03em
**Default body:** 14px, weight 400, line-height 1.57

→ Full type scale: [Visual_Design_System.md §8](./Visual_Design_System.md) and [UI_Design_System.md §4](./UI_Design_System.md)

### 2.4 Iconography

Icon library: **Lucide** (`lucide-react`), outline style, 2px stroke, `currentColor`.

Never mix icon families. Never use filled icons. Always provide an accessible label alongside icon-only elements.

→ Full icon guidelines: [Visual_Design_System.md §9](./Visual_Design_System.md)

### 2.5 Illustration Style

Flat, geometric, minimal. Maximum 3 colours per illustration from the brand palette. Used only for: empty states, onboarding, error pages, and marketing hero sections.

→ Full illustration guidelines: [Visual_Design_System.md §10](./Visual_Design_System.md)

### 2.6 Photography Guidelines

**In product:** Not used (data visualisations and YouTube thumbnails only).

**Marketing website:**
- Documentary, natural light, slightly cool and desaturated tone
- Creators in authentic working environments
- No stock photo clichés
- Product screenshots on device mockups (MacBook, browser frame)

→ Full photography guidelines: [Visual_Design_System.md §11](./Visual_Design_System.md)

### 2.7 Marketing Visuals

**Hero section:** Deep Space background, gradient headline (blue → violet), product screenshot in browser frame.

**Social posts:** Dark background, brand colours, large stat numbers or short insights, product screenshot framing.

**Email:** Light theme (white background), max 600px wide, Inter body text, Electric Blue CTA buttons.

→ Full marketing visual language: [Visual_Design_System.md §20](./Visual_Design_System.md)

### 2.8 Dashboard Branding

The dashboard is a **data canvas**. Branding is present but restrained — the data is always the visual focus, not the brand.

In-product branding elements:
- Logo in the sidebar header (icon mark on collapsed sidebar, full logo when expanded)
- Primary colour on active navigation items, buttons, and focus states
- Viral Score colour scale (the most recognisable brand data element)

**What the dashboard brand must not do:**
- Display logos or brand colours that compete with the data
- Use decorative illustration inside data-heavy pages
- Apply gradient backgrounds behind charts or tables

## 3. Content Style Guide

### 3.1 Writing Principles

**P1 — Specific beats vague**
Numbers, names, and concrete details are always better than abstract descriptions.
> ✅ "This video scored 87.4 because the hook opens with a surprising statistic."
> ❌ "This video performed exceptionally well due to strong opening content."

**P2 — Short beats long**
Cut every sentence by 20%. Then cut it again. If a word doesn't earn its place, remove it.
> ✅ "Topic is declining. Act this week."
> ❌ "Based on our analysis, this particular topic appears to be experiencing a downward trend in engagement metrics, and we would recommend taking action in the near term."

**P3 — Active beats passive**
Active voice. The subject does the action.
> ✅ "ViralScopes analysed 4,821 videos this month."
> ❌ "4,821 videos were analysed by ViralScopes this month."

**P4 — Creator-native language**
Use the vocabulary creators actually use, not marketing or technical jargon.
> ✅ "Hook", "niche", "thumbnail", "script", "sponsor segment", "end card"
> ❌ "Content introduction", "topic vertical", "visual asset", "narrative content", "monetisation segment"

**P5 — Honest and measured**
Never overstate. Confidence is earned through consistency, not enthusiasm.
> ✅ "Emerging trend — confidence: high."
> ❌ "This topic is BLOWING UP right now! 🔥"

### 3.2 Naming Conventions

| Element | Convention | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Product name | "ViralScopes.io" on first mention; "ViralScopes" thereafter | "ViralScopes.io helps creators understand..." then "ViralScopes analyses..." |
| Feature names | Title case, no "the" | "Viral Score", "Opportunity Engine", "Trend Detection", "Alert Dispatch" |
| Score name | "Viral Score" (not "virality score", "viral rating", or "VS") | "Your Viral Score is 87.4" |
| Trend statuses | Capitalised: Emerging, Evergreen, Declining | "This topic is Emerging" |
| Hook types | Title case: Question, Shock, Statistic, Promise, Story, Mystery, Fear, Curiosity, Humour | "Hook type: Statistic" |
| Plans | Title case: Free, Starter, Professional, Business, Enterprise | "Upgrade to Professional" |
| UI elements | Lowercase in sentences: dashboard, sidebar, watchlist, alert | "Open the dashboard and navigate to Watchlists" |

### 3.3 UI Copy Guidelines

**Labels and headings:**
- Always sentence case (not Title Case) for labels and body text; Title Case only for feature names and proper nouns
- Headings state what the section contains, not a question or instruction
  > ✅ "Trending Videos" ❌ "See What's Trending"
- Table column headers are short and specific: "Viral Score" not "Score (0–100 scale)"

**Buttons and CTAs:**
- Verbs that describe the action: "Analyse", "Export", "Create Watchlist", "Upgrade"
- Not vague: ❌ "OK", "Submit", "Go", "Yes"
- Destructive actions must name what is being destroyed: "Delete Watchlist" not "Delete"
- Primary CTA: one per page, the most important action

**Placeholder text:**
- Shows an example, not instructions
  > ✅ "e.g. youtube.com/watch?v=dQw4w9WgXcQ" ❌ "Enter a YouTube URL here"

**Tooltips:**
- One sentence. Explains what something is, not how to use it.
  > ✅ "The Viral Score combines 9 engagement signals to predict content performance." ❌ "Click here to learn more about the Viral Score."

**Loading states:**
- Active and specific: "Analysing thumbnail…" not "Loading…"
- Shows progress when possible: "Analysing transcript (step 2 of 5)…"

### 3.4 Error Messages

Error messages follow a three-part structure: **What happened → Why it happened → What to do next.**

```
Error message pattern:
[What happened]. [Why, if helpful]. [Action the user can take].

Examples:

Good:
"That YouTube URL wasn't recognised. Make sure the link includes /watch?v= 
 and try again."

Good:
"Analysis failed — the video has no captions available. We can still analyse 
 the thumbnail and title. [Try with partial analysis]"

Bad:
"Error 422: Unprocessable entity." (Technical, not helpful)
"Something went wrong." (Vague, no guidance)
"Invalid input." (What input? What's wrong with it?)
```

**Error tone:** Calm, non-blaming, specific, action-oriented. Never use exclamation marks in error messages. Never blame the user.

### 3.5 Empty States

Empty states follow a two-part structure: **What's empty → What to do about it.**

| Empty state | Heading | Body text | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| No videos discovered yet | "No videos here yet" | "Add a watchlist to start discovering what's performing in your niche." | "Add Watchlist" button |
| No results for search | "No results for '[query]'" | "Try different keywords, or clear your filters to see all videos." | "Clear filters" link |
| No alert rules | "No alert rules set up" | "Create an alert to get notified when a tracked channel posts or when a topic spikes." | "Create Alert Rule" button |
| No exports | "No exports yet" | "Export video analysis data to CSV, Excel, JSON, or PDF for reporting and analysis." | "Create Export" button |
| Analysis pending | "Analysing this video" | "The full analysis will be ready in a few minutes. We'll show the results here when it's done." | — |

**Empty state tone:** Encouraging, never apologetic. The empty state is an opportunity, not a failure.

### 3.6 Notification and Alert Copy

**In-app notifications (toasts):**
- Success: specific and affirming → "Watchlist 'Finance Competitors' created."
- Error: specific and actionable → "Export failed — the filter returned 0 results. Adjust your filters and try again."
- Warning: calm urgency → "You've used 85% of your monthly video quota."
- Info: neutral → "Video analysis complete. [View analysis]"

**Alert dispatch messages (Discord/Slack/email):**
Alert messages delivered to external channels must be:
- Short (under 80 words)
- Immediately actionable (link to the relevant page)
- Specific about why the alert triggered

```
Alert message template:
🎯 [Alert name] — ViralScopes

[Channel name] just posted a video scoring [score].

"[Video title]"

Hook type: [type] | Views: [count] | Posted: [time ago]

[View full analysis →]
```

### 3.7 Email Copy Guidelines

**Subject lines:**
- Specific and benefit-led: "The top-scoring video in your niche this week" not "Your weekly update"
- Under 50 characters for mobile
- No emoji in subject lines (inconsistent rendering)
- No ALL CAPS, excessive punctuation, or spam trigger words

**Email body:**
- One primary topic per email
- Opening sentence: direct, no preamble ("Here's what happened in your niche this week." not "Hi [name]! We hope you're having a great day!")
- Short paragraphs (2–3 sentences max)
- One primary CTA, clearly set apart from the body text
- Plain language — no marketing jargon

**Transactional emails (verification, reset, billing):**
- Subject: factual → "Your ViralScopes email verification link" not "Verify your email to get started! 🚀"
- Body: functional — only essential information, no promotional content
- Tone: neutral and reliable

### 3.8 Marketing Copy

**Headlines:**
- Creator-native, benefit-focused, specific
  > ✅ "Know why your next video will work before you film it."
  > ✅ "The AI that analyses 50,000 videos a day so you don't have to."
  > ❌ "Revolutionise your content strategy with AI-powered insights."

**Feature descriptions:**
- Lead with the user benefit, not the technical feature
  > ✅ "See exactly why a video scored 87 — hook type, title formula, thumbnail composition, and trend alignment."
  > ❌ "Multi-modal AI pipeline with structural pattern extraction and viral scoring algorithm."

**Social proof:**
- Specific results, not vague praise
  > ✅ "I spent 20 hours a week on research. Now it's 2." — Maya C., YouTube Creator
  > ❌ "ViralScopes is a game-changer!" — Happy Customer

**The ethical constraint in marketing copy:**
The brand explicitly communicates that ViralScopes does *not* help users copy others' content. This is a feature, not a limitation, and marketing copy treats it as such.
  > ✅ "Original recommendations only — we show you the structure, never the script."
  > ✅ "Inspired by patterns. Executed with your voice."

## 4. Accessibility

### 4.1 WCAG 2.2 AA Alignment

ViralScopes.io targets WCAG 2.2 Level AA compliance on all pages and in all email communications.

**Key standards met:**

| Criterion | Standard | Implementation |
|---|---|---|
| 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum) | 4.5:1 normal text; 3:1 large text | All text combinations tested and documented in Visual_Design_System.md |
| 1.4.4 Resize Text | 200% without loss | rem-based type scale; no fixed-height text containers |
| 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast | 3:1 for UI components | All borders, icons, and interactive elements checked |
| 2.4.7 Focus Visible | Visible focus on all interactive elements | Custom focus ring: 2px solid `#1D8CF8`, 2px offset |
| 2.4.11 Focus Appearance | Minimum size and contrast | 2px outline, 5.6:1 contrast ratio on dark bg |
| 3.1.1 Language of Page | `lang` attribute on `<html>` | `lang="en"` on all pages |
| 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value | All UI components have correct ARIA | shadcn/ui and Radix UI provide this by default |

**Accessibility testing process:**
- Automated: axe DevTools in CI pipeline on every PR
- Manual: keyboard navigation review before each release
- Screen reader: NVDA (Windows) and VoiceOver (macOS) tested on all new page templates
- High contrast: Windows High Contrast White and Black tested on all pages

### 4.2 Inclusive Language

**Language ViralScopes uses:**

| Preferred | Avoid | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "creator" | "YouTuber" (when describing all users) | Inclusive of creators on multiple platforms |
| "content" | "videos" (when referring broadly) | Not all content is video |
| "they / them" (default pronoun) | "he/she" or "s/he" | Gender-neutral default |
| "everyone", "all users" | "guys" | Gender-neutral |
| "simple", "straightforward" | "easy", "just" | Avoids assuming what is easy for everyone |
| "popular" | "viral" (in non-technical contexts) | "Viral" has specific technical meaning; use it precisely |

**Language ViralScopes avoids entirely:**
- Ableist metaphors ("blind to the data", "tone-deaf", "falls on deaf ears")
- Idiomatic phrases that don't translate ("knock it out of the park", "hit the ground running")
- Jargon without definition on first use

### 4.3 Readability Standards

- **Flesch-Kincaid grade level target:** 8th grade (accessible to the broadest audience)
- **Sentence length:** Maximum 25 words for body copy; 15 words for UI labels and notifications
- **Paragraph length:** Maximum 3 sentences in marketing and documentation
- **Acronyms:** Written out on first use — "Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR)" then "MRR"
- **Technical terms:** Defined on first use with a consistent glossary

### 4.4 Contrast Requirements

Minimum contrast ratios for all text and interactive elements:

| Element type | Minimum ratio | ViralScopes target |
|---|---|---|
| Normal text (< 18px) | 4.5:1 | 7:1 (dark theme body text) |
| Large text (≥ 18px bold or ≥ 24px) | 3:1 | 5:1 |
| UI components (borders, icons) | 3:1 | 4:1 |
| Focus indicator | 3:1 | 5.6:1 |
| Disabled text | Exempt from contrast | Marked with additional visual indicator |

## 5. Brand Governance

### 5.1 Design Approval Process

**Tier 1 — No approval needed:**
- Bug fixes and adjustments that don't change the design system
- Copy corrections (typos, grammar)
- New content following established templates (new blog post, new help article)

**Tier 2 — Engineering/product review:**
- New UI components (must be documented in UI_Design_System.md)
- Significant layout changes within existing pages
- New dashboard pages following established patterns

**Tier 3 — Design lead approval:**
- Any new design patterns not covered by the existing design system
- Changes to existing components in the component library
- New iconography or illustration additions

**Tier 4 — Founding team approval:**
- Changes to the core brand palette
- Logo modifications or new logo variations
- Changes to the brand voice or tone guide
- Major typography changes (new typefaces, headline size changes)
- Brand campaign visual direction

### 5.2 Asset Management

**Brand asset storage:** `/brand/` directory in the repository (or a dedicated Figma library linked from this document).

```
brand/
├── logo/
│   ├── logo-full-dark.svg
│   ├── logo-full-light.svg
│   ├── logo-icon-dark.svg
│   ├── logo-icon-light.svg
│   ├── logo-full-dark.png   (@2x)
│   └── favicon.ico
├── colors/
│   └── viralscopes-palette.ase  (Adobe Swatch Exchange)
├── fonts/
│   └── README.md  (links to Google Fonts; fonts not bundled in repo)
├── illustrations/
│   └── empty-states/
│       ├── no-videos.svg
│       ├── no-trends.svg
│       └── ...
└── marketing/
    ├── social-templates/
    └── email-templates/
```

**External brand assets:** The Figma brand library (link managed separately) is the source of truth for design work. All exported assets from Figma are stored in the above directory structure.

**Asset naming convention:** `[asset-type]-[variant]-[theme].[format]`
> `logo-full-dark.svg`, `illustration-empty-watchlist.svg`, `social-viral-insight-1080.png`

### 5.3 Future Brand Evolution

**Phase 1 (MVP, Current):** Brand foundation is established — logo, core palette, type system, voice, and tone. Sufficient for launch.

**Phase 2 (Month 6–12):**
- Illustration system completed (all empty states and onboarding illustrations)
- Motion design formalised and documented
- Brand photography library commissioned (3–5 hero images)
- Community brand elements (Discord server, newsletter header)

**Phase 3 (Month 18–24, Enterprise expansion):**
- White-label brand system (design token export for agency branded deployments)
- Partner and co-marketing brand guidelines
- Conference and events materials (booth, banners, print)
- Enterprise brand standards supplement (co-branding rules)

**Phase 4 (Month 30+, Platform brand):**
- Platform-specific visual identities for TikTok, Instagram analytics modules
- Marketplace brand identity
- Developer/SDK brand (documentation site, API portal)
- Multi-language brand adaptation guidelines

**Brand evolution principles:**
- The brand evolves; it does not reinvent itself. Core colours, logo, and voice are stable
- Evolution is additive — new elements are introduced to support new contexts, not to replace working foundations
- All brand evolution is documented and versioned in this file
