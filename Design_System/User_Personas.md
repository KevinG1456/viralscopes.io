## Persona 1 — The Growth-Focused Solo Creator

**Name:** Maya Chen
**Age:** 28 | **Location:** London, UK | **Subscribers:** 180,000

### Background

Maya went full-time on YouTube 18 months ago after growing her Tech & Productivity channel to 100,000 subscribers while working as a UX designer. She now earns £3,200/month from YouTube ad revenue, sponsorships, and her digital course. She manages everything alone — writing, filming, editing, uploading, and strategy.

### Role & Goals

Maya's primary goal is to grow her channel predictably. She wants to increase average views per video from 40,000 to 80,000 within 6 months. She is not interested in hacks or shortcuts — she wants to understand what actually drives performance in her niche so she can systematically improve.

**Her top 3 goals:**
1. Understand why her best videos outperform her average ones
2. Know which topics to cover before they peak (not after)
3. Spend less time on research and more time on creation

### Pain Points

| Pain | Severity | Current workaround |
|---|---|---|
| Can't identify why some videos underperform | High | Manual review; guesswork |
| Discovers trending topics after the peak | High | Checks Twitter, Google Trends sporadically |
| 5 hours of manual competitor research every Sunday | High | Notion database, manual notes |
| No framework for title/thumbnail decisions | Medium | Gut feeling + what performed before |
| Can't tell if a new topic has high or low competition | Medium | Search YouTube manually |

### Daily Workflow

```
Monday AM  → Review last week's performance in YouTube Studio
             Check notifications from ViralScopes (if set up)
Monday PM  → Plan the week's content based on research findings
Tuesday–Fri→ Film, edit, upload
Saturday   → Engage with comments, community posts
Sunday     → Research (currently 5 hours)
```

With ViralScopes: Sunday research collapses to 30 minutes.

### Technical Proficiency

Medium. Comfortable with SaaS tools (Notion, Canva, CapCut). Not a developer. Prefers visual interfaces over data tables. Values fast loading, clear labelling, and mobile-friendly layouts (checks dashboards on iPhone frequently).

### Budget

Personal spending, not business expense. Comfortable paying £39–£79/month for a tool that saves meaningful time and drives channel growth. Annual plan is attractive if the discount is > 15%.

### Decision-Making Process

Trial-first. Maya needs to see the product working on her own niche before committing. She is influenced by creator community word-of-mouth (YouTube strategy subreddits, Discord servers) and creator educator YouTube channels. She will upgrade from Free to Starter the week she runs into a quota limit on a feature she finds valuable.

### Preferred Features (Priority Order)

1. Viral Score with breakdown — understands *why*, not just the number
2. Trending topics with velocity and opportunity data
3. Original recommendations (hook ideas, title concepts)
4. Competitor channel watchlist with upload alerts
5. Weekly digest email (doesn't want to check the dashboard daily)

### Success Criteria

Maya considers ViralScopes worth the subscription if: after 3 months, her average views per video have increased by 20%+ and her Sunday research time is under 45 minutes.

## Persona 2 — The Agency Content Strategist

**Name:** Daniel Osei
**Age:** 34 | **Location:** Manchester, UK | **Company size:** 12-person agency

### Background

Daniel is Head of Content Strategy at a mid-size creator management agency that represents 8 YouTube creators across Fashion, Fitness, Finance, and Lifestyle niches. He has 6 years of content marketing experience. He reports to the agency founder and is responsible for all content strategy deliverables: weekly briefs, channel audits, competitor analysis, and creative direction.

### Role & Goals

Daniel's job is to make his clients' channels grow. His professional goal is to become indispensable by delivering better results than competitors, and his personal goal is to stop spending 20 hours a week on manual research that doesn't directly generate revenue.

**His top 3 goals:**
1. Produce better, more data-backed content strategy briefs
2. Scale the agency's output without hiring more strategists
3. Justify every recommendation with data his clients understand

### Pain Points

| Pain | Severity | Current workaround |
|---|---|---|
| 20 hours/week of manual research across 8 clients | Critical | Spreadsheets, YouTube Studio, Social Blade |
| Can't benchmark clients against competitors objectively | High | Manual view count comparison |
| Briefs are opinion-based, not data-backed | High | Adds "based on research" but can't quantify |
| No alert system — finds out about competitor posts days late | High | Subscribes to competitor channels personally |
| Different niches require completely different research approaches | Medium | Context-switching is inefficient |

### Daily Workflow

```
Monday AM  → Check what competitors posted over the weekend
             (currently: check YouTube subscriptions manually)
Monday PM  → Write content briefs for the week (2.5 hrs per client × 8 = 20 hrs)
Tue–Thu    → Client calls, creative direction, brief revisions
Friday     → Channel audit, monthly report prep
```

With ViralScopes: Monday content brief writing drops to 3 hours total (30 min per client).

### Technical Proficiency

Medium-High. Uses multiple SaaS tools daily (HubSpot, Notion, Canva, Google Data Studio). Comfortable with data tables and export functions. Wants a clean dashboard he can demo to clients without embarrassment.

### Budget

Business expense. Decision-maker for tools under £200/month. Tools above £200/month require agency founder sign-off. Strong ROI framing needed: "saves 17 hours/week of £50/hour strategist time = £850/week ROI on a £249/month tool" is a compelling case.

### Decision-Making Process

Evaluate → build case → get sign-off. Daniel will evaluate the tool for 2 weeks (free plan), identify 3 specific ways it saves time, build a business case document, and present to the founder. Product trial that shows clear time savings wins the sale.

### Preferred Features (Priority Order)

1. Multi-workspace (one per client) with separate watchlists
2. Competitor monitoring with alert dispatch to his Slack
3. Weekly alert digest (one email per client per week, not per alert)
4. Export to PDF/Excel for client presentations
5. Channel intelligence: posting cadence and average score for competitor channels
6. Opportunity Engine: data-backed content calendar suggestions

### Success Criteria

Daniel considers ViralScopes worth the subscription if: his team's weekly research time drops below 5 hours total, and at least one client reports using a ViralScopes-informed content strategy as the basis for a decision.

## Persona 3 — The In-House Brand Content Manager

**Name:** Priya Kapoor
**Age:** 31 | **Location:** Dublin, Ireland | **Team size:** 4

### Background

Priya manages the YouTube channel for a consumer tech brand (320,000 subscribers). Her team includes 2 video producers and 1 editor. She reports to the CMO and is responsible for the channel's growth KPIs: subscriber growth, average views, and video-attributed website traffic.

### Role & Goals

Priya must justify the content team's existence with business data. YouTube is a significant investment for the brand, and she needs to show the CMO that content decisions are informed by competitive intelligence, not creative instinct.

**Her top 3 goals:**
1. Build a data-backed case for content investment decisions
2. Understand how competitors are outperforming the brand's channel
3. Build a content calendar that isn't based entirely on guesswork

### Pain Points

| Pain | Severity | Current workaround |
|---|---|---|
| CMO asks for ROI data she can't easily produce | High | Manually extracts YouTube Studio data into Google Sheets |
| Can't objectively compare channel to competitors | High | Subscribes and watches manually; no systematic tracking |
| Content calendar is based on internal opinion | High | Team brainstorm + trend articles |
| Doesn't know which content formats are working for competitors | High | Watch competitor videos occasionally |
| No early warning when competitors post something significant | Medium | Subscribes to their channels |

### Daily Workflow

```
Monday     → Team standup, review last week's YouTube metrics
             Check for competitor uploads (manual)
Tue–Thu    → Production oversight, review scripts, content approvals
Friday     → Monthly report data collection (3 hours)
Monthly    → CMO performance review presentation (6 hours prep)
```

With ViralScopes: Monthly report prep drops from 3 hours to 45 minutes. CMO presentations become data-rich.

### Technical Proficiency

Medium. Comfortable with Google Analytics, YouTube Studio, and reporting tools. Not technical. Needs clear labels, no jargon, and shareable outputs (export-ready reports).

### Budget

Company budget, not personal. Must submit a tool justification. Budget owner is the CMO. At £89–£249/month (Professional/Business), can be approved as a single line item. Needs ROI framing.

### Decision-Making Process

Priya researches independently, builds a one-page tool comparison, and presents to the CMO with an ROI estimate. The CMO approves tools under £300/month without a formal procurement process. The trial period (14 days on free plan) is her evaluation window.

### Preferred Features (Priority Order)

1. Channel comparison: brand vs 5 competitors, objective viral score benchmarking
2. Export to Excel/PDF for CMO presentations
3. Competitor upload alerts to team Slack
4. Opportunity Engine: data-backed content calendar for the quarter
5. API access (if the data team wants to pipe it into internal Tableau)

### Success Criteria

Priya considers ViralScopes worth the subscription if: within 60 days, she has used ViralScopes data in at least one CMO presentation, and the CMO has referenced it in a content decision.

## Persona 4 — The Startup Founder

**Name:** Marcus Williams
**Age:** 38 | **Location:** Birmingham, UK | **Company:** B2B SaaS startup

### Background

Marcus co-founded a B2B SaaS startup 2 years ago. He runs a YouTube channel as the primary brand-building and inbound lead channel. With 45,000 subscribers, the channel generates 30% of his inbound leads. He does his own content strategy but has hired a video editor.

### Role & Goals

Marcus wants to use YouTube as a growth lever without hiring a dedicated content strategist. He needs to be efficient — spending no more than 2 hours per week on strategy — while still producing content that outperforms his competitors in the SaaS space.

**His top 3 goals:**
1. Grow YouTube channel to 100,000 subscribers by end of year
2. Increase inbound leads from YouTube by 50%
3. Spend < 2 hours/week on content strategy

### Pain Points

| Pain | Severity | Current workaround |
|---|---|---|
| Limited time — channel is one of 5 priorities | High | Delegates less research than he should |
| Can't identify which video formats drive qualified leads, not just views | High | Correlates manually with CRM data |
| No visibility into what SaaS creator competitors are doing | Medium | Occasionally watches competitor videos |
| Doesn't know if he should create topic X before or after a competitor | Medium | Checks YouTube search results |

### Technical Proficiency

High. Founder background means he's comfortable with data, APIs, and SaaS tools. Would use the API to connect ViralScopes data to internal dashboards if it was available. Early adopter of new tools.

### Budget

Business expense with no formal approval process. £39–£89/month is trivially easy. Would upgrade to Professional for API access once v1.5 ships.

### Decision-Making Process

Fast. Marcus sees value quickly and commits fast. He is likely to find ViralScopes via Twitter/X or a creator newsletter, sign up for free, view the Viral Score on a competitor's video, and upgrade within 48 hours.

### Preferred Features (Priority Order)

1. Viral Score with structural breakdown (title formula, hook type)
2. Competitor channel watchlist for SaaS creators
3. API access (v1.5) to pipe into internal dashboards
4. Trend detection: emerging SaaS creator topics

### Success Criteria

Marcus considers ViralScopes worth the subscription if his next 3 video topics come from ViralScopes Opportunity Engine data and at least one significantly outperforms his previous average.

## Persona 5 — The Enterprise Media Director

**Name:** James Thornton
**Age:** 45 | **Location:** London, UK | **Organisation:** National news publisher

### Background

James is Director of Digital Content at a major UK national publisher. His YouTube strategy sits within a 30-person digital team. He oversees YouTube channels for Politics, Culture, Sport, and Tech verticals — each with a separate editorial team. He tracks 50+ competitor channels and reports to the Chief Digital Officer.

### Role & Goals

James needs enterprise-grade intelligence infrastructure. His challenge is not analytical skill — he has excellent data instincts — but systematic monitoring at scale with access control for multiple teams.

**His top 3 goals:**
1. Systematic, automated monitoring of 50+ competitor channels across 4 verticals
2. Per-vertical access control — Tech editors should not see Politics watchlists
3. Data pipeline to internal Tableau dashboards via API

### Pain Points

| Pain | Severity | Current workaround |
|---|---|---|
| 50+ competitor channels with no systematic monitoring | Critical | Editorial teams manually monitor their patches |
| No unified intelligence view across all verticals | High | Separate spreadsheets per team |
| No API access to pipe data into internal tools | High | Manual data export and re-import |
| No RBAC — any tool used is shared without access control | High | Separate accounts per team |
| SLA requirements for any external tool | High | Most tools have no SLA |

### Technical Proficiency

High. Works closely with the data team. Understands APIs, BI tools, and data pipelines. Would require a technical integration review before purchase.

### Budget

Enterprise budget, formal procurement process. Annual contracts require procurement approval. £12,000–£36,000/year annual contract is within his delegation authority. Needs a formal DPA, SLA, and security review.

### Decision-Making Process

Slow (6–12 weeks). Involves IT security review, procurement, legal DPA review. The DPA and SLA are non-negotiable. A dedicated account manager for onboarding is expected. James is not the contract signer — the CDO or Chief Commercial Officer is.

### Preferred Features (Priority Order)

1. Multi-workspace with RBAC (one workspace per editorial vertical)
2. Public API with sufficient rate limits for data team
3. SLA-backed uptime (99.95%)
4. DPA and security documentation
5. Dedicated account manager
6. Competitor monitoring across 50+ channels
7. Alert dispatch to team Slack channels per vertical

### Success Criteria

James considers ViralScopes successful if: within 3 months, all 4 editorial teams are using their workspace, the data team has a live Tableau integration, and the CDO has referenced ViralScopes data in a digital strategy meeting.
